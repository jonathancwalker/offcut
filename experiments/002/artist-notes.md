# Rootstock for a Small Planet

An inverted tower supports a flower whose seed is Earth. A lunar shelf crosses the specimen like a soil section; the fine roots below it make architecture briefly behave like something that can grow.

The collage uses all three supplied sources: the marsh marigold plate, the Flatiron photograph, and Apollo 8's Earthrise. The black polygon is also a fragment of Earthrise, cut from its empty sky. All artwork changes were made through the provided action API.

## Observable revisions

- The first assembly established seven fragments: the black field, lunar shelf, inverted tower, Earth, a yellow flower, and two leaves.
- A second rendering joined those forms with a dissected flower cup and a stalk. The yellow bloom moved toward the upper left, the leaves moved toward the tower, and Earth became the flower's seed.
- The final visual revision raised the lunar shelf and brought it in front of the tower, making a clear ground section. Fine botanical roots were added beneath the inverted cornice. The right leaf became smaller, and Earth settled deeper into the yellow cup.
- The final exported PNG was inspected after saving the title and statement.

## Counts and constraints

- 10 fragments used of 24 available.
- 32 actions used of 120 available, including the final description.
- No artwork action was rejected; no fragments were removed.
- The initial attempt to reach the local worktable was blocked by sandbox networking. The permitted local connection succeeded afterward.
- The polygon vertex limit leaves visible angular cuts and some source-paper edges. There were no generated pixels, added sources, filters, imported projects, resets, undo/redo operations, or changes to the engine.
